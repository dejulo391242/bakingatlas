3:2:1 — swap the ingredients

Pie crust → shortbread: keep 300 g flour and 200 g butter; replace 100 g water with 100 g sugar. Cut cold butter into flour for flaky crust; mix softened butter with sugar for crumbly cookies.

Pie crust → biscuit: reverse water and butter quantities (200 g milk, 100 g butter per 300 g flour). Add baking powder. More liquid and less fat make a dough that can rise and form a soft interior. Scones often add egg and sugar.

Related, not identical

Lean bread and pizza share a flour-water-salt-yeast base; pizza may add oil. Focaccia usually increases water and oil. Bagels reduce water and are boiled before baking. Puff pastry and croissants both laminate butter, but croissants use yeast. Muffins and pancakes share flour, milk and egg proportions in these examples; muffins use more fat and sugar.

Replacing wheat flour

ApplicationReplacement considerations
Cookies / shortcrustStart with an equal weight of a GF blend intended for cookies or pastry. Chill before shaping; add water in small increments if crumbly.
Cakes / muffinsUse a GF baking blend with binder. Rest batter briefly to hydrate; adjust milk if unusually thick.
PancakesUse a GF blend; add milk gradually until batter pours easily.
Yeast bread / pizzaUse a dedicated GF bread formula, not a direct wheat substitution. Psyllium and higher hydration help hold gas; dough may resemble thick batter.

Blend ingredients

Rice flour: neutral base; sorghum: flavor and body; potato starch: tenderness and water retention; tapioca starch: cohesion and chew. Almond and coconut flour are not direct 1:1 substitutes.

Example blend — 500 g

200 g white rice flour + 150 g brown rice flour + 100 g potato starch + 50 g tapioca starch. This blend contains no binder; use an application-specific binder amount.

Binders and hydration

Check commercial blends for xanthan gum before adding more. Xanthan helps cookies and cakes hold together; psyllium is useful in bread. They are not interchangeable gram for gram. Add liquid gradually, allow time for absorption, then assess consistency.

Use labeled gluten-free ingredients and avoid cross-contact from shared flour, utensils and surfaces.
